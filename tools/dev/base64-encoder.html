Frequently Asked Questions

Base64 is a binary-to-text encoding scheme that represents binary data in ASCII text format. It's commonly used to encode images in HTML/CSS (data URIs), transmit binary data over JSON, or encode files for email attachments.
No. The encoding and decoding is done entirely in your browser with JavaScript. Your data never leaves your device.
Base64 encoding increases size by approximately 33%. A 100KB image becomes ~133KB when Base64 encoded. For web performance, consider using actual image files over data URIs for anything over a few kilobytes.

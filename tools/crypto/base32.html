❓ FAQ

What is Base32?
Base32 is a binary-to-text encoding that uses a 32-character alphabet (A-Z and 2-7). It's commonly used in applications that need case-insensitive encoding, such as DNS records and certificate fingerprints.
What's the difference between Base32 and Base64?
Base32 uses 32 characters (A-Z, 2-7) and is case-insensitive. Base64 uses 64 characters (A-Z, a-z, 0-9, +, /) and is case-sensitive. Base32 produces longer output but is more reliable in environments where uppercase/lowercase distinction is lost.

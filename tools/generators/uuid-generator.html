Frequently Asked Questions

A UUID (Universally Unique Identifier) is a 128-bit number formatted as a 36-character string like 9b1deb4d-3b7d-4bad-9bdd-2b0d7b3dcb6d. UUIDs are used to uniquely identify entities in software systems without requiring a central authority.
UUIDs come in several versions. Version 4 generates random UUIDs using cryptographically secure random numbers. It's the most commonly used version for general-purpose unique identifiers. The format is xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx where y is always 8, 9, A, or B.
UUID v4 has 122 bits of randomness (~2^122 possible UUIDs). That's approximately 5.3 undecillion (5.3×10^36) unique UUIDs — more than enough for any practical application. The chance of a collision is effectively zero.
